Microplastics in Municipal Water - Executive Technical Report

Role: Water Safety Director (Simulation)
Focus: Source-to-Tap Pathways, Filtration Boundaries & Policy Directives
Author: Pranjal Sonkiya (B.Tech CSE Core, 1st Year)

Executive Technical Summary

Microplastics represent an emerging environmental and water-safety concern across municipal drinking-water systems. Contaminants enter water sources through plastic degradation, stormwater runoff, industrial discharge, wastewater effluents, and atmospheric deposition. Secondary contamination also occurs through aging distribution pipelines.

Treatment Capabilities & Technical Constraints

While conventional municipal processes—such as coagulation, flocculation, sedimentation, and granular media filtration—can arrest larger particles, their retention efficiency drops substantially with sub-micron plastic fragments and synthetic fibers. Standard chemical disinfection protocols do not eliminate synthetic polymers.

Actionable Policy Timeline

  • 0–6 Months: Mandate standardized source-to-tap sampling, baseline spectroscopic testing, and granular filtration audit.
  • 6–12 Months: Implement stricter industrial effluent discharge caps and municipal stormwater containment barriers.
  • 12–24 Months: Establish national reporting standards and pilot advanced membrane barrier filtration at treatment plants.
